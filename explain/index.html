How SDMP explains a layout

Every SDMP embedding is one matrix product, Y = U · P, and everything on the explanation page reads one of its three parts. Here it gets built up one factor at a time, on a dataset small enough to read cell by cell.

1. The three parts

The blocks are drawn to shape. U is as tall as the dataset and as wide as the modes kept, while P holds two numbers per mode, which are the only thing optimization changes.

2. U, what the graph looks like at each scale

A column of U is a picture over the graph, rather than one number per point.

3. P, how much of each scale the layout uses

Row s of P is two numbers, so it works as a direction in the plane. It is the way mode s pushes a point that has a positive value in that mode. The arrows are drawn to their own scale.

Each arrow is one row of P.

Response magnitude per mode, called G2 in the paper.

4. The product, one point at a time

A point's position is the sum of its eight displacements, yn = un,1p1 + … + un,8p8. Laid end to end from the origin, they land exactly on the point. Click to pin one.

L1 above and L2 below. Together they make one row of the U·P panel.

5. How to misread this

  • A large |un,s| does not mean mode s matters to the layout. It matters only against ‖ps‖. That is why the viewer has both panels. U shows what the graph makes available, and U·P shows how much of it the layout used.
  • A mode index is not a feature index. Only the subspace is determined, and close eigenvalues mix neighbouring modes. Reading a band of modes is safer than reading a single column.
  • An eigenvector's sign is arbitrary. Only the product un,sps means anything, and the signs you see here come from a convention rather than from the data.
  • Cutting the chain at K gives YK in the sense of the reconstruction error, meaning the final coefficients truncated. A run that stopped at K modes would have landed somewhere else, since it re-optimizes at every stage.
